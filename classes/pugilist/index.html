Pugilist

Pugilists live rough lives that leave them full of determination and reckless confidence, whether that confidence comes from desperation, overconfidence, or hard experience. In a fight, they channel that strength D&D Portal - Strength Physical power and athletic ability Strength measures physical power, athletic ability, and a character's capacity to exert force. Full link: https://dnd-portal.com/rules/ability-scores/strength/ of character into moxie and use it to survive enemies with greater numbers, arms, or armor.

A Pugilist is not a disciplined monk or polished weapon master. The class is built around back-alley brawling, improvised pressure, pain tolerance, unarmed strikes, and a refusal to stay down when a normal combatant would already be finished.

At the table, the Pugilist wants to close distance, trade blows, spend moxie at the right moment, and let a Fight Club define the exact shape of their brawling style, from theatrical arena work to dirty tricks, sleuthing, grappling, boxing, or fighting beside a loyal hound.

Class Features

As a pugilist, you gain the following class features.

Fisticuffs

Level 1

At 1st level, your years of fighting in back alleys and taverns have given you mastery over combat styles that use unarmed strikes and pugilist weapons, which are simple melee weapons without the two-handed property, whips, and improvised weapons. What is a Pugilist in D&D?

The Pugilist version used by D&D Portal is a Strength- and Constitution-focused d8 martial class built around Fisticuffs, Iron Chin, Moxie, improvised or pugilist weapons, and a never-stay-down style of fighting. It gains Moxie at 2nd level, Bloodied but Unbowed and a Fight Club at 3rd level, Extra Attack and Haymaker at 5th level, and increasingly strong durability features at higher levels. This is not the later d10 Pugilist (2024) version.

How do you play a Pugilist?

Play a Pugilist by keeping Strength and Constitution high, using Fisticuffs as your reliable baseline, and spending Moxie according to what the round needs. Brace Up improves durability, The Old One-Two increases offense, and Stick and Move improves mobility or control. Bloodied but Unbowed can restore momentum after heavy damage, while Haymaker is a deliberate accuracy-for-damage tradeoff rather than an automatic attack mode.
