Messenger FAQ

What is the Messenger class in D&D?

The Messenger is a 10-level calling from *The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying*, published by Free League. It represents an experienced traveller, advisor, and bearer of important tidings. The class uses a d8 Hit Die, has Dexterity and Charisma as its primary abilities, and combines mobility, broad skill use, journey-focused features, Sneak Attack, and the Counsellor or Herald path.

The Messenger is a playable calling from *The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying*, published by Free League. It represents an experienced traveller, advisor, and bearer of important news: someone accustomed to crossing long distances, dealing with different peoples, and reaching places where information or assistance is needed. Mechanically, the Messenger combines mobility, broad competence, social ability, precise weapon attacks, and journey-oriented features. It is not a heavily armoured front-line class by default, nor is it built around spellcasting. Instead, it succeeds by being difficult to pin down, useful in many different situations, and capable of reaching the right place at the right time. Unlike standard Player's Handbook classes that normally progress to level 20, the Messenger is a 10-level calling within *The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying* character system.

Core class profile

The Messenger uses a d8 Hit Die.

Its primary abilities are Dexterity and Charisma, and it is proficient in Dexterity and Charisma saving throws.

These ability scores reflect the two main sides of the class.

Dexterity supports the Messenger's physical agility, initiative, finesse or ranged weapon attacks, and several useful skills.

Charisma supports the character's role as an envoy, advisor, and negotiator.

It also interacts directly with some Messenger mechanics, including Unarmoured Defence.

The class is therefore designed around a character who is both physically mobile and socially capable rather than one who specializes exclusively in combat.

The Messenger's armor, weapon, and skill proficiencies are those listed in the calling's class profile.

Only proficiencies explicitly granted by the Messenger calling are part of the class.

What makes the Messenger different?

The Messenger is defined by several features that reinforce the same central idea: this is a character accustomed to travelling, adapting, and operating between different groups of people.

At 1st level, features such as Folk-lore, Tireless and Swift, and Unarmoured Defence already establish that identity.

Folk-lore represents the knowledge accumulated through exposure to different peoples, customs, traditions, and stories.

Tireless and Swift makes mobility and endurance part of the class rather than merely flavour.

Unarmoured Defence connects the Messenger's two primary abilities by allowing Dexterity and Charisma to contribute to defense under the feature's equipment requirements.

Later features add precise combat damage through Sneak Attack, broader practical competence through Errand Runner, selective defense through Uncanny Dodge, and additional customization through Virtues and Crafts.

Instead of giving the class one dominant resource that is pressed every turn, the Messenger builds its strength from several complementary tools.

Mobility and travel

Travel is central to the Messenger's identity.

Tireless and Swift improves the character's ability to move and endure the demands of travelling, with the feature developing further at higher Messenger levels according to its progression.

In combat, additional movement can be extremely useful even though it does not deal damage directly.

It can help the Messenger reach a useful enemy, move between allies, reach cover, avoid wasting a turn simply closing distance, reposition for a better ranged attack, create a better opportunity for Sneak Attack, or carry assistance or information from one part of an encounter to another.

Outside combat, the same theme supports a character intended to spend significant amounts of time on the road.

Tireless and Swift has concrete mechanical benefits: at 1st level it increases speed by 5 feet while the Messenger is not wearing heavy armour and adds the Messenger level to fatigue saving throws made at the end of a journey. The speed bonus increases at 6th and 10th level, and the 10th-level improvement also helps the Messenger recover from exhaustion during short rests.

Combat role

A standard Messenger is better understood as a mobile skirmisher than as a heavy front-line warrior.

Sneak Attack rewards the character for making a qualifying finesse or ranged weapon attack under the correct circumstances.

This makes target selection and positioning important.

The Messenger is often interested in making one valuable attack from a good position rather than simply standing beside an enemy and exchanging attacks until somebody falls.

Its increased movement supports that approach.

At later levels, Uncanny Dodge provides another layer of defense by allowing the Messenger to reduce damage from a qualifying attack using its reaction.

Together, mobility, Sneak Attack, and Uncanny Dodge create a combat style based on choosing positions and reducing risk rather than absorbing enormous amounts of damage.

The Herald path can significantly change this role by making the Messenger more martially capable.

Skills and practical competence

The Messenger is intended to contribute well beyond combat.

A traveller carrying information between distant communities is likely to face many different kinds of problems, and the class mechanics reflect that broad experience.

Errand Runner expands the Messenger's usefulness on ability checks where the character does not already benefit from full proficiency.

Errand Runner adds half the Messenger's proficiency bonus, rounded down, to any ability check that does not already include the proficiency bonus.

The important consequence is that the Messenger does not become useless when an ability check falls outside the small number of skills in which the character is most specialized.

Folk-lore similarly gives the Messenger a distinct role when knowledge about peoples, customs, stories, traditions, or related subjects becomes important.

That makes the class particularly useful during exploration, travel, investigations, social encounters, councils, and adventures involving unfamiliar communities.

Unarmoured Defence

The Messenger's Unarmoured Defence uses:

10 + Dexterity modifier + Charisma modifier

while the character satisfies the feature's equipment restrictions.

This is an unusual and important interaction because Charisma is not only a social ability for the Messenger.

Improving Charisma can also improve the character's defense when using Unarmoured Defence.

Dexterity simultaneously supports Armor Class and the weapon attacks commonly used with Sneak Attack.

This creates deliberate synergy between the Messenger's main ability scores.

Unarmoured Defence is an option rather than a requirement. A Messenger can instead use permitted armour when that produces a better defensive setup, and Herald expands the armour options available to the character.

Virtues and Crafts

The Messenger progression also includes choices involving Virtues and Crafts from *The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying* character system.

These choices provide customization beyond the fixed Messenger features.

They allow two characters with the same calling and even the same Messenger Path to develop different strengths.

The Messenger gains a Virtue at 2nd, 4th, 6th, 8th, and 10th level. At 2nd, 6th, and 10th level, the character can take a Craft instead, giving the calling regular customization throughout its ten-level progression.

Virtues and Crafts use the *The Lord of the Rings Roleplaying* rules and are not interchangeable with ordinary D&D feats.

Messenger Paths

At 3rd level, the Messenger makes its largest specialization decision by choosing a Messenger Path.

The two published paths used here are:

  • Counsellor
  • Herald

Counsellor develops the Messenger toward advice, social expertise, councils, communication, and helping allies.

Herald develops the Messenger toward martial leadership and more direct weapon combat.

Counsellor adds social expertise and stronger ways to advise or assist allies, while Herald adds martial proficiencies, a Fighting Style, a Charisma-based initiative bonus, and later Extra Attack.

Both paths continue using the same core Messenger foundation.

Choosing Herald does not stop the character from being a traveller, envoy, or skillful Messenger.

Choosing Counsellor does not remove the character's movement, Sneak Attack, or other combat capabilities.

The path determines which part of the class becomes more prominent.

How the ten-level calling develops

The Messenger progresses over ten levels.

The early levels establish travel and cultural knowledge, mobility, Unarmoured Defence, Sneak Attack, Virtues or Crafts, broad ability-check competence, and the Messenger Path.

Later levels improve or expand those systems rather than replacing the class with an unrelated play style.

The Messenger progression table gives the exact level-by-level timing, while the important milestones are summarized here in prose.

Because the calling ends at 10th level, those milestones arrive quickly: the Path is established at 3rd level, Uncanny Dodge arrives at 5th, and later levels improve mobility, customization, and the chosen Path.

What the Messenger contributes

The Messenger is valuable because it can contribute in several different phases of play.

During exploration and journeys, mobility, Folk-lore, skills, and broad practical competence support the group.

During social encounters, Charisma and the class's envoy identity allow the Messenger to participate meaningfully in negotiation and communication.

During combat, Sneak Attack and movement reward careful positioning while Uncanny Dodge provides selective protection.

The chosen Messenger Path then determines whether the character leans more strongly into support and interaction or into weapon combat.

This makes the Messenger a flexible specialist rather than a character with only one narrow job.

In short

The Messenger is a mobile, adaptable calling built for a character who travels between people rather than remaining rooted in one place.

Dexterity and Charisma form its mechanical foundation.

Features such as Folk-lore, Tireless and Swift, Unarmoured Defence, Sneak Attack, Errand Runner, Virtues or Crafts, Uncanny Dodge, and the Messenger Paths build on that foundation across its ten levels.

A Messenger works best when the player uses movement, skills, social interaction, positioning, and the character's Path together rather than treating the class as a weaker version of a conventional front-line warrior.
