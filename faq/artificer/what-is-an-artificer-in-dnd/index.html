Artificer FAQ

What is an Artificer in D&D?

An Artificer is an Intelligence-based half-caster who combines magic, tools, crafted equipment, and magical infusions. Rather than relying only on spells, Artificers turn ordinary objects into magical resources, support the party with flexible utility, and specialise at 3rd level as an Alchemist, Armorer, Artillerist, or Battle Smith.

An Artificer is a magical inventor: a character who treats arcane power as something that can be understood, shaped, stored, and applied through tools and objects. The class is neither a conventional full spellcaster nor a purely martial character. Instead, it combines prepared spellcasting with tool expertise, magical equipment, and features that reward planning ahead. Mechanically, the Artificer uses a d8 Hit Die and has proficiency with light armor, medium armor, shields, simple weapons, and several tools. Intelligence is the class's central ability score. It determines Artificer spellcasting and powers several later class features, making a high Intelligence score useful in almost every part of the class. The result is a class that excels at flexibility. An Artificer can improve equipment, solve exploration problems, contribute healing or control magic, support important saving throws, and develop into a much more specialised combat role through its Artificer Specialist.

A half-caster with prepared spells

Artificers gain Spellcasting at 1st level, but their spell-slot progression is slower than that of full casters such as Wizards, Clerics, and Bards.

Spellcasting should therefore be treated as one part of the Artificer rather than the entire class.

Artificers prepare spells from the Artificer spell list using Intelligence.

After a long rest, the Artificer can change which spells are prepared, allowing the character to adjust to the expected adventure.

That is an important difference from a class that permanently knows only a small selection of spells.

The Artificer spell list provides a mixture of utility, protection, restoration, battlefield support, control, and magical problem-solving.

Specialists then add additional capabilities and can change how heavily the character relies on direct spellcasting.

Artificer spellcasting is also strongly connected to tools and objects.

The class uses tools and, later, infused items as spellcasting focuses. This supports the idea that an Artificer's spells can be represented as crafted devices, alchemical reagents, runes, mechanisms, magical components, or other inventions.

Infusions are one of the class's defining mechanics

At 2nd level, Infuse Item allows the Artificer to turn suitable nonmagical objects into magic items using learned Artificer Infusions.

This is one of the most important class features because it changes the equipment available to both the Artificer and the party.

Depending on the chosen infusion, an Artificer may be able to improve a weapon, improve armor, enhance a spellcasting focus, reproduce the effect of a useful magic item, provide specialised utility, or give equipment to another party member.

An Artificer knows more infusions than can be active at one time.

That distinction is important.

Knowing an infusion gives the Artificer the option to use it.

The number of infused items the character can actually maintain determines which of those options are currently active.

This makes infusion selection a form of preparation similar to preparing spells.

A good Artificer does not only ask, “What infusion is strongest?”

They should also ask, “Which character in this party benefits most from this item today?”

Tools matter mechanically

Tools are central to the Artificer's identity.

The class begins with multiple tool proficiencies and later gains Tool Expertise, which doubles the proficiency bonus for qualifying ability checks using tools.

That makes the Artificer particularly useful in campaigns where tools are used for things such as opening locks, disabling mechanisms, analysing constructed objects, repairing equipment, crafting, alchemy, investigation, and identifying materials or workmanship.

The exact ability check still depends on the situation and the DM.

Tool Expertise does not make every technical problem an automatic success.

It makes the Artificer unusually reliable when one of their tool proficiencies actually applies.

Flash of Genius and party support

At 7th level, Flash of Genius becomes one of the Artificer's strongest support features.

When the Artificer or another creature the Artificer can see within the feature's range makes an ability check or saving throw, the Artificer can use their reaction to add their Intelligence modifier to the roll.

The number of uses is also connected to Intelligence.

This means Intelligence improves much more than spell attacks and spell save DCs.

A high Intelligence score can also help an ally resist a dangerous effect, another character pass an important saving throw, someone succeed on a critical ability check, or the Artificer improve an important tool or skill check.

Because Flash of Genius uses a reaction and has limited uses, timing matters.

It is usually most valuable when failure has serious consequences or when the Intelligence bonus has a realistic chance of changing the result.

Magic items become increasingly important

As the Artificer gains levels, magic items become even more deeply integrated into the class.

Magic Item Adept, Magic Item Savant, and Magic Item Master improve how the Artificer interacts with attunement and magic items.

Spell-Storing Item allows the Artificer to place a qualifying low-level Artificer spell into a suitable object.

A creature holding that object can then produce the stored spell effect according to the feature's rules.

This is especially useful because the Artificer can effectively hand part of their magical toolkit to another party member.

At 20th level, Soul of Artifice directly links the character's survival to their magical equipment and infusions.

By this point, an Artificer's equipment is not simply loot.

It has become part of the class itself.

Artificer Specialists

At 3rd level, an Artificer chooses an Artificer Specialist.

The four main published Specialists in the pre-2024 class are:

  • Alchemist
  • Armorer
  • Artillerist
  • Battle Smith

These choices can make two Artificers play very differently.

Alchemist emphasises alchemical magic, experimental mixtures, and support.

Armorer turns magical armor into the centre of the character's combat style.

Artillerist uses constructed magical artillery and ranged magical pressure.

Battle Smith combines weapon combat, magical support, and a Steel Defender.

The subclass therefore determines whether the Artificer feels more like a support caster, armored magical combatant, arcane artillery specialist, or weapon-using inventor.

What role does an Artificer fill in a party?

Artificers rarely have only one job.

They can provide magical utility, healing, defensive buffs, improved equipment, tool expertise, saving-throw support, ranged or melee combat, and subclass-specific companions or constructs.

Their main strength is adaptability.

A party without many magic items can gain immediate value from infusions.

A party struggling with saving throws can benefit from Flash of Genius.

A campaign with frequent traps, mechanisms, crafting, or investigation gives the Artificer's tools more room to matter.

The Artificer is therefore particularly effective for a player who enjoys thinking about the party as a whole rather than only their own turn.

In short

An Artificer is an Intelligence-based magical engineer whose power is spread across spells, tools, magical equipment, support features, and subclass mechanics.

The class is strongest when the player enjoys preparation and adaptation.

Instead of asking only, “What spell should I cast?” an Artificer often asks, “What should I prepare, what should I infuse, who should receive this item, and which tool or class feature solves the current problem most efficiently?”
