Monk FAQ

How do you play a Monk?

Play a Monk as a mobile skirmisher: keep Dexterity and Wisdom high, use movement to reach useful targets, and spend ki according to the situation. Flurry of Blows increases offense, Patient Defense improves survival, Step of the Wind improves mobility, and Stunning Strike can control a priority enemy. Avoid treating every ki point as automatic damage or standing still like a heavily armored Fighter.

Playing a Monk well is largely about ki economy and positioning. The class has enough movement to choose fights that other melee characters cannot reach as easily, but it has a d8 Hit Die and does not normally use conventional armor. That means mobility should be treated as part of defense.

Build around Dexterity and Wisdom

Dexterity is normally the Monk's highest ability score.

It improves:

  • qualifying Martial Arts attacks;
  • Armor Class;
  • initiative;
  • Dexterity saving throws;
  • common mobility skills.

Wisdom contributes to Unarmored Defense and the Monk's ki save DC.

Constitution is usually the next priority because the Monk still needs enough hit points to survive melee combat.

Understand your bonus action

The Monk has strong competition for the bonus action.

Depending on the turn, the bonus action may be used for:

  • the normal Martial Arts unarmed strike;
  • Flurry of Blows;
  • Patient Defense;
  • Step of the Wind;
  • subclass features.

This means not every option can be used simultaneously.

Before taking the action, decide what the turn actually needs.

If the Monk is surrounded and vulnerable, Patient Defense may matter more than two extra strikes.

If reaching the objective is more important than damage, Step of the Wind may be correct.

If a target must fall quickly, Flurry of Blows may be worth the ki.

Do not spend all ki on Stunning Strike

Stunning Strike is powerful.

It is also easy to overuse.

A target with a strong Constitution saving throw may consume several ki points without being stunned.

Those same points could have been used for movement, defense, Flurry of Blows, or subclass features.

Use Stunning Strike when the target is important enough that the potential control is worth the resource.

It becomes especially attractive when the party can exploit the stunned condition before it ends.

Use mobility to choose targets

The Monk's speed is one of its strongest advantages.

Good targets may include:

  • enemy spellcasters;
  • isolated ranged attackers;
  • creatures threatening a vulnerable ally;
  • enemies that need to be reached across difficult positioning;
  • objectives that require someone to cross the battlefield quickly.

Do not assume the Monk should attack the nearest enemy merely because that enemy is available.

The class is often valuable because it can reach the enemy other characters cannot.

Deflect Missiles is positioning insurance, not a reason to ignore cover

At 3rd level, Deflect Missiles allows the Monk to use a reaction to reduce damage from a ranged weapon attack.

If the damage is reduced to 0, the feature can sometimes allow the Monk to catch the projectile and spend ki to make a return attack when the requirements are met.

This is useful protection against ranged pressure.

It does not make the Monk immune to ranged attacks.

Cover, line of sight, and positioning remain important.

Know when Patient Defense is stronger than more attacks

Patient Defense spends 1 ki point to Dodge as a bonus action.

Dodging can dramatically improve survival when several attack rolls are likely to target the Monk.

That may create more value than one additional offensive burst.

A living Monk still contributes next round.

The correct decision depends on incoming threats rather than a fixed rotation.

Let the Tradition shape the play style

Open Hand adds control to Flurry of Blows and reinforces direct martial technique.

Shadow adds magical stealth and infiltration tools.

Four Elements spends ki on elemental disciplines, increasing pressure on the same resource pool used by base Monk abilities.

Kensei supports dedicated weapon mastery.

Mercy adds healing and harm options.

Astral Self changes how the Monk approaches reach and ability dependence through subclass mechanics.

The subclass should change how ki and positioning are managed.

Common mistakes

Common Monk mistakes include:

  • spending ki until empty in the first encounter;
  • attempting Stunning Strike against every target regardless of Constitution;
  • forgetting Patient Defense;
  • standing still beside enemies when movement could create a safer position;
  • spreading ability scores too widely;
  • wearing armor and accidentally disabling major class features;
  • forgetting that several important features compete for the bonus action;
  • treating the Monk as a conventional damage sponge.

A strong Monk uses speed and ki to control when and how the character is exposed to danger.
